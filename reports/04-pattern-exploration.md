**These correlations are not evidence on their own.** All three passes rate
the *same* items, so the per-family means correlate across passes even if
family carries no information: item-level agreement is enough. Null: shuffle
the family labels across items (1,000 times; each item keeps its three
scores) and recompute. Null mean Spearman: median 0.351, 95th percentile
0.626. Observed 0.604, permutation p = 0.069.
The observed stability is not above the null at the 95th percentile, though close to it: the ranking as a whole is not reliably reproduced beyond what shared items alone produce.

Two things survive anyway. Across all three passes, among n >= 10 families,
top 3 every time: `K1_cancellation`;
bottom 3 every time: `I_intensified`.
The middle does not hold: the largest rank shift between passes is
`M_modal` 7, `B_quantifier` 6, `H_hedged` 6, `A_syntactic` 4 places.
And the disagreement is mostly about sentence order, not batch neighbours: passes 1
and 3 (same A-B order, different neighbours) correlate at 0.811; pass 2 (B-A
order) correlates with them at 0.545 and 0.455.

## Part D — verdict

**The family CIs mostly overlap and the per-pass family rankings are not reliably more stable than shuffled family labels give: apart from its two ends, the pattern does not survive the noise.** Only 35% of family pairs (n >= 10) have disjoint CIs;
per-pass ranking stability is 0.604 against a shuffled-label
null whose 95th percentile is 0.626 (p = 0.069); agreement
on the negation pairs alone is 0.365.

Does a graded alpha look justified? On these labels a per-family alpha fits
better than any constant: CV MAE difference (per-family − best flat)
-0.0515 on label/5, 95% CI [-0.0675, -0.0342]. That gain is
real *for this labeller* and not an artefact of one sentence (Part B8). But it
is a fit to the labeller's floor. 61% of negation pairs are at 0, and
family means track the share at 0 with r = -0.958. What the formula
learns is mostly *which families the labeller calls completely dissimilar*.
The only structure that survives every check is at the ends: cancellation
high, `I_intensified` low.
That high end comes from the clean `never X to` cancellations (mean
4.20); K1's family mean is pulled *down* by a generator defect in its
affixal subtypes (Part A4).

Would human labels confirm it? Most likely they would overturn it. The rubric puts
same-topic pairs at 2. A rater following it would not be expected to put
61% of negations, which share every content word with their
base, at "completely dissimilar". Lift the floor and the family ordering, which
here is mostly floor rate, has to be re-established from scratch; every alpha
(best flat 0.303) would rise, by an amount these labels cannot tell us. The ends
(cancellation near no-negation, intensified negation furthest away) are the
part most likely to survive, because they also match the linguistic expectation.

What would change this answer:
- a relabel (one pair per prompt) whose negation-only alpha reaches >= 0.6 (now 0.365);
- the human seed agreeing with these labels at Krippendorff alpha >= 0.6;
- on labels that pass: family means whose correlation with the floor share falls
  well below |r| = 0.9 (now 0.958), a per-family − best-flat CI still
  entirely below zero, and per-pass ranking stability above the permutation
  null's 95th percentile (now 0.604 vs 0.626).
